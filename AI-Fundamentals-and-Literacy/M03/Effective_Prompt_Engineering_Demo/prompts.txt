Prompt 2:


You are assisting a CTO at a company that is planning its AI investments.

TASK:
Identify the three biggest AI-related risks or cautions raised in the attached report.

CONTEXT:
The CTO needs to decide where to be cautious before committing budget. Base your analysis only on the attached report.

AUDIENCE:
A senior technology leader who wants a brief, decision-ready view.

FORMAT:
Present your findings in a table with these columns:
- Risk or caution
- What the report says (include page numbers)
- Strength of evidence (hard figure cited / expert opinion only)
- Suggested next step

Rank by how often or how strongly the report emphasises each point.

Use a concise, professional tone. Do not add statistics or claims that are not in the report. Mark clearly where something is a prediction rather than a measured result.


Prompt 3:

You are assisting a Learning & Development manager at a mid-sized company.

TASK:
Identify the three most important findings in the attached report that should shape our upskilling plan.

CONTEXT:
The manager must decide where to focus a limited training budget. Base your analysis only on the attached report.

AUDIENCE:
A non-technical L&D manager who needs actionable business insights, not technical detail.

FORMAT:
Present your findings in a table with these columns:
- Finding
- Supporting evidence (include page numbers)
- Evidence type (survey or platform data / author prediction)
- Recommended action

Rank the findings by relevance to upskilling decisions, most relevant first.

Use a concise, professional tone. Do not invent facts, statistics, or causes that are not in the report. Clearly distinguish what the report states from your recommendations.


Prompt 4: 

You are assisting a security manager at an online retailer.

TASK:
Summarise the cybersecurity predictions in the attached report and identify which two are most relevant to a retailer.

CONTEXT:
The manager is preparing a short briefing for leadership. Use only the attached report. If the report does not address retail directly, say so rather than assuming.

AUDIENCE:
Non-technical executives.

FORMAT:
- A table with columns: Prediction | Evidence cited (with page) | Why it matters to a retailer | Suggested action
- Then a 3-sentence plain-English summary for executives.

Use a concise, professional tone. Do not invent facts or figures. Label the "why it matters" and "suggested action" columns as your own analysis, not the report's.
